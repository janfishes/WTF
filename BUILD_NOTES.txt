Build 2026-07-31 · 10:03 PM EDT · v250 · Left-right wag
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The splash tail's two sweeps now read as different sides. v248's pure
foreshortening compressed the tail identically on both halves of the beat,
so in silhouette it looked like the tail only ever flapped toward the
viewer. A side-on silhouette genuinely cannot tell left from right by shape
alone, so the two half-beats now carry the two classic perspective cues:
the near-side sweep scales up 6% (closer looks bigger) and holds full
white; the far-side sweep scales down 7% and dims a touch toward the
ground color (farther looks smaller and hazier). The tail's fill animates
independently of the body's — the shared path's fill moved onto each <use>
so the tail's copy could take keyframes; the spot's black is now set
explicitly so it stops inheriting. Same joint, same overlap, same
reduced-motion opt-out (now covering both animations).

Build 2026-07-31 · 9:57 PM EDT · v248 · Sideways wag
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The splash tail now wags side to side instead of bobbing. v245's motion was
an in-plane rotation, which on a fish drawn in profile reads as the tail
flapping up and down; a real tail sweeps toward and away from the viewer.
In profile that shows as foreshortening, so the rotation is replaced with a
scaleX pulse about the same joint: the tail compresses to 70% of its length
at each side of the sweep (twice per 1.9s beat) and stretches back to full
as it crosses the body plane, with a whisper of skewY (±2.2°) in opposite
directions on the two half-beats so near-side and far-side sweeps read
differently. Same articulated SVG, same 10px overlap at the joint (scaleX
pulls the tail's cut edge INTO the overlap, so no seam), same
reduced-motion opt-out. The About sheet copy inherits it as before.

Build 2026-07-31 · 9:43 PM EDT · v245 · Tail wags
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The splash redfish wags its tail. The artwork is no longer the 11 KB base64
PNG — it is an SVG traced off that PNG's own pixels (silhouette, the knocked-
out WTF lettering, and the tail spot as a fitted circle), about half the
bytes, and crisp at any size. The tail is its own group inside the SVG,
rotating ±2.4° at the caudal peduncle (the narrow point ahead of the fin,
found by scanning the PNG's alpha for the thinnest column). Two copies of the
one fish path are clipped either side of the joint with a 10 px overlap, so
the wag never opens a seam. The wag's keyframes and its
prefers-reduced-motion opt-out live inside the SVG itself, so anywhere the
src is reused wags too — the About sheet picks it up automatically. The
header's small logo keeps its own separate PNG and does not wag. The v219
whole-fish swim (bob + roll) is retired in this build — with the tail
beating, the bob on top read as two motions fighting — so the wag is now the
splash's only movement. Net effect on the file: about 5 KB smaller.

Build 2026-07-31 · 8:07 PM EDT · v239 · Moon phase logged
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every catch now shows the moon phase for the moment it was caught: the small
line-art moon glyph (the same one the Bite sheet draws), the % lit, and
whether the moon was waxing or waning — e.g. "82% waxing". It appears on the
date/tide line of the My Log cards (and the stacked-diamond popup that reuses
them) and in the single-catch map popup's meta row.

Nothing new is stored on the catch record. The phase is recomputed at render
time from the catch's date and time with the on-device lunar astronomy the
Bite forecast already uses (catchMoonStr), so it fills in for every existing
catch, imports included, and stays correct if a date is edited. A catch
without a time is read at noon — the lit fraction moves ~0.4% per hour, so
the worst case is off by a couple of percent. New Moon and Full Moon say
"new"/"full" instead of a direction. Works fully offline.

Build 2026-07-31 · 8:00 PM EDT · v237 · Popups stay visible
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fixes the Waves popup opening with its top half buried under the filter row,
seen on both the Mac and the iPhone. Two separate timing holes, both closed:

1. The Waves button (and the search jumps to a spot or a logged catch) opened
   the popup on a fixed 600-650 ms timer racing map.flyTo(), whose real
   duration scales with the distance flown and routinely runs past a second.
   When the timer won, the popup opened mid-flight: the auto-pan cleared it,

   the popup's top edge behind the filter row. All three jumps now open the
   popup on the map's moveend (the real "flight over" signal), with an

   and a timer kept only as a backstop (flyThenOpen()).

   clear for THAT height; the data rows landing a moment later roughly
   triple it, and a popup grows upward from its anchor — straight back under
   the filter row. Leaflet only auto-pans at open, so every swap of the buoy
   popup's body (data, cached data, failure message) now re-runs the pan by
   hand (repanBuoyPopup()), reusing the furniture padding already measured
   at open. A popup being dragged has autoPan off, so the re-pan never
   fights a finger.

Verified headless in Chrome at iPhone (390x844) and Mac (1440x900) sizes:
after the flight and the content swap, the popup's top edge sits below the

Build 2026-07-31 · 7:40 PM EDT · v236 · Compass self-heals
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hardens the compass redraw so it can no longer go dark for a whole session.
The redraw used to clear the overlay, draw the route legs, and add the rose
last — so if any one leg threw mid-draw, the overlay was left completely
blank, and because the route lives only in memory the same failure replayed
on every hover, tap, pan and zoom until the page was reloaded. That is the
likely shape of the "dashed lines missing, legs not working on the Mac"
report against v235 (the page itself was current — reloading fixed it while
all saved data survived, which points at wedged in-memory route state, not
the code or the cache).

Three changes, all in redrawCompass(): the rose is drawn FIRST, so no leg
failure can ever blank it; each leg draws inside its own try/catch, so a bad
leg loses only its own line while the rose and the other legs still land;
and any route entry without finite coordinates is dropped from the route
itself at the top of the redraw, so a poisoned route heals on the next draw
instead of wedging until reload. No visual or behavioural change when
everything is healthy.
The map's own text now rides the A text-size control at the buttons' half
strength, like the filter pills and compass text: the pin name labels
(inshore, offshore and launch pills shown when Labels is on) and the count
badges on reef pins and merged catch diamonds — a catch diamond's count is
the only text it shows on the map. The badge box grows with its numeral so
the number can't burst the circle at 130%. The compass overlay's collision
layout measures name-tag widths in px, so its measuring font (and the
tag's half-height keep-out) carry the same factor. Help's Text Size note
updated to match.

Build 2026-07-31 · 4:58 PM EDT · v226 · Man overboard
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Press and HOLD Locate Me (about a second — the pill floods red while it
arms) to drop a MAN OVERBOARD mark and start guiding back to it. A small
red human figure now rides on the Locate Me pill to mark the hold function;
the shape carries the meaning, the red carries the urgency.

What a press gets you: a MOB mark at your position — the FIRST fix
available, immediately, upgraded in place if a sharper GPS fix lands within
twelve seconds of the press (the opposite tradeoff from Locate Me, which
waits for quality; a boat at 20 kt covers a hundred metres in ten seconds,
so here fast-and-rough wins). The mark is a red figure on a white disc in a
DASHED red ring, pulsing. A red banner sits over everything with the
bearing to steer (magnetic, like every bearing in the app), the distance
(feet under ~1000 ft, statute miles beyond), and a clock since the press —
the clock is what tells the crew how wide the drift circle has grown. A
dashed red line runs boat→mark, updated from a high-accuracy GPS watch,
with your live position shown alongside. The screen holds awake while MOB
is active (wake lock, re-acquired on returning to the foreground).

The mark is written to localStorage synchronously the instant it exists, so
a Safari reload or phone restart mid-recovery brings the banner and
guidance straight back on load. Tapping the banner fits boat and mark in
view. CLEAR asks first, then keeps the mark in your spots as
"MOB <date> <time>" — the record you'd hand the Coast Guard.

Long-press mechanics: pointer events, 12 px of wander cancels (that's a
drag), releasing early cancels, the after-hold click is eaten by a capture
listener, and the context menu is suppressed on the pill. Holding again
while active just re-centres and says to CLEAR first — no accidental
re-marks.

Help gets a Man Overboard section (quick-tour line plus a full note under
Locate Me) with the two honest cautions: the mark is where the PHONE was
at the press, not where the person went in, and people drift — this is an
aid to memory, not a rescue device; the drill is still a pointing spotter,
flotation, and the VHF.
