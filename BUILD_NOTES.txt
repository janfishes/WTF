Build 2026-08-03 · 3:25 PM EDT · v370 · Track Recording
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
New TRACK button (above Locate Me): tap to record the boat's path as a
magenta breadcrumb line on the map, tap again (or STOP on the bar) to
finish. A slim white bar at the top — accent border, non-blocking, the
chart stays fully usable — shows distance covered, elapsed time, and,
where the browser allows it, a live battery %. Points are GPS-filtered
(fixes rougher than 35 m dropped; a point lands after 12 m of real
movement, 5-decimal rounding) and written to localStorage one by one,
so an iOS backgrounding reload resumes the recording instead of losing
it. Past 4000 points the track thins itself (every other point, spacing
doubles). Recording needs no signal — GPS works offshore; only imagery
needs tiles. A wake lock holds the screen awake; recording pauses when
the app leaves the screen (iOS gives web apps no background location),
and the bar says so. Battery: Chrome/Android get a live % plus a
low-battery toast at 20%; Safari (Mac + iPhone) removed the Battery
Status API, so there the start toast and the bar's foot line carry the
drain warning instead. Kept tracks (last 10, device-local, not in
backups) live under "Your tracks" in + Spot with SHOW/DELETE; a kept
track stays drawn after STOP; deletes go through UNDO and the 14-day
Recently-deleted bin like spots and catches. Tapping the bar fits the
map to the track so far. While a MARK is up, its banner owns the top of
the screen and the track bar steps aside; the recording continues.

Also in this build, at Jan's request: the MARK feature's internal names
finally match its face — every mob identifier, .mob- class, and the
wtf:mob key renamed to mark forms (v361 had changed only the user-facing
words). Two legacy shims keep old data honest: a wtf:mob key left by a
pre-v370 build migrates on load, and pins saved with the old mob:true
flag still paint red beside the new mark:true.

Build 2026-08-02 · 2:46 AM EDT · v326 · Purple tiles really fixed
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The purple aerial tiles came back after v318 — bigger, and on the Mac too.
Measurement showed why: the same USGS request answers magenta about one time
in four and natural color the rest. One render node in USGS's load-balanced
image cluster is drawing four-band NAIP photos in false-color infrared, and
no request parameter avoids it (v318's bandIds, the NaturalColor rendering
rule, and the old WMS endpoint all measured equally affected). Worse, the
app's tile cache kept whatever arrived first, so one bad answer poisoned
that tile on the device until the offline map was cleared — that's why the
Mac showed "major purple."

The fix is client-side: every NAIP tile is now decoded and inspected before
it reaches the map. A tile whose pixels run magenta (red and blue both well
above green — no natural land or water scene does that in quantity) is
refetched through a cache-busting request until a healthy server answers,
up to four extra tries, which drops the odds of purple ever rendering below
one in two hundred. The good copy replaces any bad one stored in the tile
cache, so poisoned caches heal themselves as tiles are viewed — nothing to
clear by hand. The offline downloader runs NAIP tiles through the same
guard, so a pre-downloaded Hi-Res pack can't bake a purple tile in. If all
tries come back bad the tile still shows (a purple photo beats a hole) but
is evicted from the cache so the next visit tries again.

  Inshore   #1636ff blue on Mosquito Lagoon (#515848) ............. 1.06:1

1.0:1 means two colours are identical in brightness. The purple and the blue
were within about one percent of the water they were sitting on.

Offshore is the easy one, because seaward of the state-waters line the app
paints a single flat #295890 and nothing else. Amber #FFC400 is the complement
of that blue and lands at 4.5:1.

Inshore is harder. Its ground is two different things at once — near-black
river water at one end and white chart and shoal areas at the other, often in
the same screen — so no single fill can be high-contrast against both. Light
beats the water and loses to the white; dark does the reverse. The best a
single colour can do is sit where the two curves cross, at luminance 0.289,
which yields 3.10:1 in both directions. Magenta #FF3DD0 (luminance 0.292) is
that point almost exactly, and magenta has a second property that mattered
here: no water anywhere is ever magenta. Cyan, teal and blue all read as water
on a chart, which ruled out the whole cool side of the wheel except this.

Amber and magenta are also about 270 degrees of hue apart, so the two zones
stay distinct at 14px, in glare, and for red-green colour blindness — which
the old blue-against-purple pair did not, especially through the amber
polarized lenses the Brown Sugar theme is built for.

Launches move off #8a4b0f to the dark bitter chocolate #4f1d0a. Nothing was
wrong with the brown itself; the problem was that v300 borrowed it from Brown
Sugar's theme accent, and #catchToggle.active paints itself with that same
accent via var(--teal). Under Brown Sugar the Catch pill and the Launches pill
therefore came out byte-for-byte identical. The two are now independent values
and --launch must never be set back to a theme accent. #4f1d0a carries no blue,
so it still holds its own colour under amber lenses, and takes white at 15.1:1.

Measured across all six themes, lit Catch pill against lit Launches pill:

  Parrot Head ..... 7.03:1     Tequila Sunrise .. 5.36:1
  Blue Suede ...... 7.48:1     Purple Haze ...... 4.63:1
  Green-Eyed Lady . 6.77:1     Brown Sugar ...... 2.05:1

Brown Sugar is the honest weak spot and worth writing down. Its accent IS a
brown, so any brown launch colour stays somewhat close to it under that one
theme. #4f1d0a is about as far as brown can go — the first attempt, #6e2f12,
only reached 1.49:1 — but 2.05:1 means the two pills are distinguishable
rather than unmistakable. The root fix is to stop painting #catchToggle with
the raw theme accent and give Catch a fixed colour of its own. That would also
repair the Catch pill's white-on-accent text, which is failing in five of the
six themes (1.86:1 on Blue Suede, 1.98:1 Parrot Head, 2.05:1 Green-Eyed Lady,
2.59:1 Tequila Sunrise, 3.00:1 Purple Haze; only Brown Sugar's dark accent
passes, at 6.79:1). That change is not made here — it is a separate call.

One consequence worth recording. Both new zone fills are much lighter than the
colours they replace, so white chip text no longer works on them: white sits at
1.60:1 on the amber and 3.07:1 on the magenta, against 6.99:1 on the old blue.
The lit Inshore and Offshore chips now take the near-black #04141e instead —
6.0:1 on magenta, 11.6:1 on amber — the same near-black --on-accent already
uses wherever a light accent needs text on top of it. The dark text-shadow was
dropped on those two chips as well, since it existed to lift WHITE text off a
bright ground. Launches keep white text, being the one dark fill of the three.

The off-state chips got simpler at the same time. Off state is text and outline
on a black chip, so the old blue and purple each needed a lifted variant
(#6b8cff, #b478ff) to stay legible there. Magenta reads 6.83:1 on black and
amber 13.1:1, so both now use the zone colour itself and the unlit chip finally
matches the pin it refers to. Launches still need their lift and keep #b5713a.

Catch pins are untouched. They are filled per species from the SPECIES table
and are separated from spots by SHAPE — a rotated diamond against a circle —
not by colour, so the zone change does not reach them.

WHY NOT FETCH FWC DIRECTLY: myfwc.com sends no Access-Control-Allow-Origin
(verified 2026-08-01) and X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN, so a browser at
janfishes.github.io can neither fetch nor frame it -- and there is no FWC
regulations API; the numbers live inside Umbraco-rendered tab panels. Server
side there is no such wall, which is how Build 283's audit was done. So the
scraping stays on a schedule off-device and the app reads its OWN origin.

HOW IT WORKS: regs.json sits beside index.html (GitHub Pages, same origin --
and it also serves Access-Control-Allow-Origin: * , so a future native
wrapper can read it too). The REGS table baked into the build is the offline
fallback. On open the app applies the last copy it cached in localStorage,
then fetches regs.json cache-busted and applies THAT if its `updated` date is
newer. Comparison is on a parsed MM/DD/YY date, never string order. A file
that fails to parse, arrives with fewer than 10 species, or carries an older
date is IGNORED -- a bad file must never blank the limits. Failure is silent
by design: offline you keep the baked-in table, not an error.

The chart is now built by renderSeasonChart(), called again whenever the
table is replaced, so nothing may hoist REGS values into a const at parse
time. BUILD_REGS_ASOF holds what the build shipped with; when the live file
is newer the panel prints a teal line saying the limits were refreshed after
the build, which is the one thing a reader cannot tell by looking.

REGS_CHANGELOG.md added: one entry per publish of regs.json, old value, new
value, and the FWC page it was read off.

Build 2026-07-31 · 7:40 PM EDT · v236 · Compass self-heals
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hardens the compass redraw so it can no longer go dark for a whole session.
The redraw used to clear the overlay, draw the route legs, and add the rose
last — so if any one leg threw mid-draw, the overlay was left completely
blank, and because the route lives only in memory the same failure replayed
on every hover, tap, pan and zoom until the page was reloaded. That is the
likely shape of the "dashed lines missing, legs not working on the Mac"
report against v235 (the page itself was current — reloading fixed it while
all saved data survived, which points at wedged in-memory route state, not
the code or the cache).

Three changes, all in redrawCompass(): the rose is drawn FIRST, so no leg
failure can ever blank it; each leg draws inside its own try/catch, so a bad
leg loses only its own line while the rose and the other legs still land;
and any route entry without finite coordinates is dropped from the route
itself at the top of the redraw, so a poisoned route heals on the next draw
instead of wedging until reload. No visual or behavioural change when
everything is healthy.
The map's own text now rides the A text-size control at the buttons' half
strength, like the filter pills and compass text: the pin name labels
(inshore, offshore and launch pills shown when Labels is on) and the count
